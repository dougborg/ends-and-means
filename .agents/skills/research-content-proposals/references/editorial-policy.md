# Editorial policy

## Evidence standard

- Prefer primary records, official statistics, peer-reviewed research, academic
  books, and authoritative institutional publications. Use reputable secondary
  sources to synthesize or locate primary evidence, not to manufacture consensus.
- Cite the exact public URL used for each empirical claim. Record title,
  publication date, access date, publisher/identifier provenance, source type,
  and why the source is authoritative. Browse each URL and verify that it opens
  the cited work. Deterministic validation cannot establish authority or whether
  a page genuinely supports a claim. Never invent a
  citation or imply that a source supports more than it does.
- Separate observations that can be checked (`empirical`) from judgments about
  desirable ends, acceptable trade-offs, or interpretation (`value-judgment`).
  A sourced opinion remains a value judgment.
- Actively seek serious counterevidence. `conflictingEvidence` must explain what
  was found and link its relevant claims, or describe the search and why none was
  located. `limitations` must identify boundaries, uncertainty, and missing data.
- Quote sparingly. Paraphrase accurately and retain enough provenance for a
  reviewer to verify the interpretation.

## Neutrality and scope

Describe mechanisms, conditions, outcomes, and failure modes. Do not treat a
system label as a verdict, generalize from one case without qualification, or
hide disputes behind false balance. State uncertainty plainly.

## Editorial authority

A proposal is research input, not published truth. The skill may create or
revise files only under its proposal directory. It must not promote, merge, or
copy material into canonical content. Human editorial review owns acceptance,
ID changes, reconciliation with existing records, and promotion.

Run the repository's canonical graph validation before proposal validation.
Proposal validation then checks compatible cross-links against the generated
canonical graph; it does not insert a proposal into that graph or simulate
promotion.
