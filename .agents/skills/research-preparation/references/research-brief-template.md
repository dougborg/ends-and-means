# Research brief template

Copy this structure into the implementation issue body. Keep the marker,
version, preparation date, disposition, and applicable section markers stable;
rewrite headings and guidance into concise issue-specific prose.

<!-- research-brief:v1 -->

Prepared: YYYY-MM-DD

Disposition: `ready` | `needs-evidence` | `deferred`

Brief scale: `substantial` | `short-correction`

## Reader outcome

<!-- research-brief-section:reader-outcome -->

State the concrete question the reader should be able to answer and the limits
of the promised explanation.

## Scope and boundaries

<!-- research-brief-section:scope-boundaries -->

Name meanings, place, period, institutions, affected people, and exclusions.
Preserve the issue's original scope and distinguish unresolved boundaries.

## Existing records and dependencies

<!-- research-brief-section:existing-records-dependencies -->

List checked canonical IDs, semantic duplicates, active Research Obligations,
open changes, and implementation dependencies. Explain each reuse or non-match.

Use `inspected`, `lead`, or `inaccessible` for State. Only `inspected` evidence
may support a candidate claim. Preserve recording, translation, archive,
publisher, and access mediation where relevant.

Distinguish rules, attributed proposals or rationales, interactions, conditions,
and outcomes. Keep local inference explicit and do not infer practice from
design or chronology from an undated passage.

## Disagreements and comparison opportunities

<!-- research-brief-section:disagreements-comparisons -->

Record serious counterevidence, rival interpretations, source conflicts, and
specific comparison questions. Do not force a score, equivalence, or inherited
classification.

## Cases, narrative, and optional visuals

<!-- research-brief-section:cases-narrative-visuals -->

Outline bounded Cases or Episodes, the learner-facing narrative sequence, and
optional visual candidates. Keep illustrative meaning separate from factual
evidence and record unresolved rights questions.

## Gaps and disposition

<!-- research-brief-section:gaps-disposition -->

Separate blocking evidence gaps, representable limitations, targeted follow-up,
and implementation dependencies. Explain why the disposition above follows.

## Implementation acceptance

<!-- research-brief-section:implementation-acceptance -->

Translate the original issue scope and reviewed mapping into executable checks.
Retain source-fitness, source-similarity, semantic, rendered, independent-review,
and release gates that apply to the eventual content change.

The brief documents preparation. It is not canonical content, a source-fitness
verdict for uninspected material, Project promotion, publication approval, or a
substitute for the implementer's final passage and mapping review.
