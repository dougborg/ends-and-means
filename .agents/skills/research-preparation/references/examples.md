# Research brief scaling examples

These examples demonstrate disposition and scale. They are structural examples,
not historical claims or reusable evidence.

## Short correction

<!-- research-brief-example:short-correction -->

Use `Brief scale: short-correction` when one existing proposition, citation, or
date needs a bounded repair. Record the affected canonical ID, the exact old and
corrected proposition, the inspected manifestation and locator, downstream
narrative or relationship projections, and focused acceptance checks. Omit
unrelated substantial-tranche sections rather than filling them with boilerplate.

Example disposition: `ready` only when the corrected proposition is supported
by an inspected passage and every affected projection is identified.

## Inaccessible source

<!-- research-brief-example:inaccessible-source disposition:needs-evidence -->

An inaccessible catalog result or discovered citation may have title, author,
date, URL, and a promising abstract or snippet. Record it as `inaccessible`,
state the access failure and attempted route, and keep the dependent claim out
of the evidence-complete set. The brief disposition is `needs-evidence` when
that claim is material to the issue and no inspected alternative supports it.

Populating every bibliographic field does not change the evidence state. Do not
quote or paraphrase a passage that was not consulted, infer one edition's text
from another manifestation, or route around access controls.
