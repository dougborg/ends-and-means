Anarchism is a disputed family of ideas and movements that oppose governing domination; it does not mean the absence of organization, and no single design or historical episode represents the whole family.

## meaning

Anarchists have challenged the state and other durable hierarchies while offering conflicting answers about ownership, exchange, durable organization, and routes to social change.
The shared name marks a field of argument, not one institutional blueprint.

## organization

Anarchist proposals commonly replace centralized rule with voluntary associations, assemblies, delegates, and federations.
Rocker's anarcho-syndicalist proposal gave unions two tasks: defend workers in present conflicts and prepare federated worker administration.
That is one pathway, not a program inherited by every anarchist current.

## disputes

Anarcho-communist, syndicalist, mutualist, individualist, insurrectionist, and organizational-dualist currents differed over exchange, distribution, mass organizations, and strategy.
Their overlaps with socialism and communism require historical qualification rather than automatic parentage.

## spain

From July 1936 to 1939, anarchist-led initiatives operated alongside socialist organizations, political parties, regional governments, and wartime state institutions in Republican Spain.
Mujeres Libres organized autonomously because broader commitments to emancipation had not removed women's subordination within the movement.
This evidence shows practice and internal limits under civil war; it does not make Spain an embodiment of anarchism or establish transfer to other places and periods.
