Populism is a disputed way of naming politics that constructs a morally privileged people against an elite.
Researchers analyze that pattern as an ideology, discourse, style, or strategy, and some reserve the term for claims that one actor alone represents the real people.
The label cannot be inferred from popularity, redistribution, charisma, nationalism, or criticism of elites by itself.

## definitions

Mudde calls populism a thin-centered ideology built around a pure people, a corrupt elite, and the people's general will.
Weyland instead centers a personalistic leader who wins or exercises power through direct support from largely unorganized followers.
Aslanidis treats populism as a discursive frame, while Moffitt analyzes a performed style that joins people-versus-elite appeals to staged crisis and norm-breaking manners.
Müller adds a narrower democratic warning: anti-elitism becomes anti-pluralist when a claimant says only they represent the real people.

These definitions select different units of analysis and can classify the same speech, movement, party, leader, or government differently.
Every use of the label therefore needs a named classifier, definition, evidence, period, and scope.

## people-and-elite

“The people” may mean citizens, workers, producers, a nation, an ethnic group, a majority, ordinary people, or another constructed constituency.
“The elite” may be political, economic, cultural, technocratic, or foreign.
Those boundaries determine who can belong, speak, contest a decision, or be excluded, so a people-claim is not innocent merely because it invokes popular sovereignty.

## democracy

Rovira Kaltwasser argues that populism can press neglected demands into public debate and can also threaten pluralism and liberal-democratic restraints.
The two possibilities must be investigated rather than read off the label.
Criticism of elites does not prove an exclusive claim to representation, and charisma does not prove authoritarian rule.

## peoples-party

The United States People's Party organized an agrarian challenge in the 1890s.
Its Omaha Platform opposed producing people to concentrated financial and corporate power and proposed public control of railroads and communications, monetary reform, graduated taxation, and ballot protections.
The 1896 platform reaffirmed the earlier program while participating in a changed electoral strategy around William Jennings Bryan.

These national platforms show attributed claims and proposals, not the experience of every farmer, worker, Black organizer, or woman associated with the movement.
They also do not turn one party bearing the Populist name into a definition for other places and periods.

## peronism

Argentina's 1949 constitutional settlement joined Peronist self-description to newly enumerated protections for labor and social welfare.
In a separate April address and associated text, Perón framed an organized community as an alternative to both unconstrained individualism and materialist collectivism.
Those records establish Justicialist self-description and formal design; they do not by themselves decide whether ideology, discourse, style, strategy, or anti-pluralism best explains the formation.

The 1943–1955 case cannot stand for every later Peronist organization or government.

## zambia

Resnick classifies Michael Sata's 2006 Zambian campaign as a populist strategy that joined anti-elite appeals to urban poor voters with a broader coalition effort.
Her account reports attacks on incumbent elites and Chinese investors over labor conditions and exclusion.
National Assembly debate from early 2007 also locates the Patriotic Front within opposition-party bargaining rather than government rule during this period.

This case tests one strategy definition; poverty, foreign-investment criticism, and personal leadership remain insufficient classifications on their own.

## thailand

Thaksin's 2001 government policy statement proposed village and urban funds, farmers' debt relief, and expanded health care within a wider program.
Phongpaichit and Baker argue that his populism developed after taking office through changing rhetoric and increasingly personalized authority.
The later classification therefore depends on more than the policies themselves.

Thai uses of ประชานิยม (prachaniyom) require their own linguistic and political context rather than automatic equivalence with every English definition.

## comparisons

Begin by separating the speaker, organization, audience, claim, institution, and observed result.
Then ask who counts as the people and the elite, whether the label is self-applied or external, which definition supplies the criteria, and whether participation or exclusion changed in practice.
Compare formal rules, rules-in-use, rights, opposition, distribution, and accountability without turning these dimensions into a single populism score.
