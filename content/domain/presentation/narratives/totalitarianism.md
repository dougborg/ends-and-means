Totalitarianism is a contested analytical category for projects that seek unusually comprehensive ideological and organizational domination.
It is neither a neutral synonym for dictatorship nor proof that any regime achieved total control.

## What does the classic regime type claim?

Linz's ideal type combines a monistic power center, an exclusive guiding ideology, and extensive mobilization.
Arendt's different analysis treats Nazi and Stalinist domination as a novel form rather than merely a stronger traditional dictatorship.

## Why do scholars dispute the category?

The term became a major Cold War comparison between Nazi and Soviet regimes, and critics challenged both its explanatory value and its ideological uses.
Different theories can therefore select different mechanisms even when they discuss some of the same cases.

## How did the label travel?

Amendola used totalitarian language to criticize Fascist monopolization of power, while Mussolini later appropriated it.
Its history includes self-description, external classification, scholarly analysis, and polemical extension.

## What can the 1933 German case establish?

Nazi leaders destroyed plural government, made the Nazi Party the sole legal party, and legally linked party and state during 1933.
Their coordination drive reached deeply into organized life without proving literally complete control or settling every definition of totalitarianism.
