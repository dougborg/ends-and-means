Communism can name an intended classless society, a revolutionary tradition or movement, a party identity, or institutions claimed by a government.
Those meanings overlap historically, but none proves that the others are present.

## what-can-communism-mean

A social ideal describes a destination; a program proposes how to reach it; a movement or party organizes people; and a bounded case records what institutions did in a named place and period.
Keeping those referents separate makes disagreements within communism visible and prevents a political label from deciding what the evidence shows.

## what-did-marx-and-engels-propose

For Marx and Engels in 1848, communist politics shared a near-term objective with other workers' parties: political rule by the proletariat after it had organized as a class and displaced bourgeois supremacy.
Their property claim targets capital used to command wage labor, not personal appropriation for a person's own life.
They propose making capital common property so productive wealth loses its class character.

## did-communists-agree-on-the-path-or-destination

Marx's initial communist phase retains limits inherited from capitalism and relates individual consumption to contributed labor after common deductions.
He conditions a higher phase on greater productive capacity and an end to subordination by the division of labor.
Only then does he propose distribution according to need.
Lenin argues for a coercive proletarian state during a transition and expects it to become unnecessary after class domination ends.
Kropotkin instead joins communism to anarchism, common possession, provision by need, and direct organization outside parliament.

## was-communism-one-global-movement

Geoff Eley's account follows early communist organizing across several regions, including parts of Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and Europe.
In his interpretation, Manabendra Nath Roy and José Carlos Mariátegui adapted revolutionary programs while challenging parts of Comintern strategy.
That interpretation does not substitute for locally authored scholarship about any region.

## does-a-communist-label-settle-the-case

A communist party name or a state's self-description establishes what an actor called itself.
It does not establish how property, class relations, distribution, political authority, or the state worked in practice.
Those questions require evidence from bounded places, institutions, and periods rather than treating a country as a timeless embodiment.
