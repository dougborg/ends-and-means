Capitalism is not simply trade, markets, private belongings, or the pursuit of profit.
It is a disputed name whose definitions emphasize different combinations of property, employment, firms, finance, and production for exchange.

## what-defines-capitalism

There is no uncontested checklist that makes an economy capitalist.
For Geoffrey Hodgson, extensive employer–employee relations and an advanced credit system belong to the minimum institutional bundle.
Karl Marx locates the capitalist’s increment beyond exchange, in the use of the worker’s purchased capacity to labor.
Karl Polanyi distinguishes land, labor, and money from commodities produced for sale.
These definitions overlap, but they ask different questions and should remain attributed rather than blended into a false consensus.

## which-institutions-work-together

Legal property differs from possession, and Hohfeld’s analysis separates several kinds of legal position.
Employment gives an employer authority to direct work.
Firms coordinate much of their internal activity administratively rather than through market transactions.
In Hodgson’s account, capital records a transferable asset’s monetary valuation.
His definition also treats a legal system supporting property and contract as necessary.

## why-markets-are-not-enough

Markets and paid labor existed long before the institutional transformation commonly associated with modern capitalism.
Market exchange can coexist with ownership forms other than private ownership.
Production for exchange does not by itself establish who owns productive assets.
Calling every market arrangement capitalist would erase the very institutional differences the label is meant to help compare.

## what-do-bounded-cases-show

Robert Brenner attributes England’s agrarian development to agrarian class relations, but other historians dispute his account.
The Gold Coast cocoa boom began through African initiative and redirected resources already in use elsewhere.
After 1978, Chinese enterprises received more discretion and new competitors appeared even though planning continued.
The cases therefore provide different, bounded observations rather than a single origin sequence.

## what-remains-disputed

Definitions disagree about which combination of institutions is necessary and about where capitalism begins historically.
The Brenner debate contests Brenner’s explanation of agrarian change.
Naughton presents the reform sequence as an incremental departure from planning rather than an immediate rupture.
These disputes cannot be settled by assigning a timeless capitalist identity to a country.

## how-to-compare-capitalism

Compare property and possession, authority in employment, coordination inside firms, finance, and market rules separately.
Keep the comparison bounded to a specified place and period.
