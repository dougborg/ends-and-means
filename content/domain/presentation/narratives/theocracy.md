Theocracy is a contested classification for government in which divine sovereignty, authoritative religious law, or a religious office has legally consequential governing authority.
It does not follow from a population's faith, an official religion, sacred language, or religious participation in public debate.

## meanings

The word has named divine rule, priestly government, and modern constitutional arrangements that join ordinary state institutions to binding religious authority.
Rule by clergy is therefore one narrower possibility, while constitutional theocracy describes a hybrid rather than the disappearance of constitutions, elections, or courts.

## boundaries

Classification requires evidence about powers: who interprets religious law, whether review binds legislators, who appoints or removes officeholders, and where executive, judicial, electoral, and coercive authority sits.
An established faith, a religious-law clause, sacred monarchy, or organized religious influence answers only part of that inquiry.
Theocracy and authoritarianism can overlap, but they identify different institutional features.

## iran

Iran's amended Constitution assigns public affairs to elections and separates legislative, executive, and judicial branches, yet subjects all law to Islamic criteria and places the branches under the Leader's supervision.
The Guardian Council reviews legislation and supervises elections; the Assembly of Experts selects the Leader; and the Leader holds enumerated policy, military, appointment, and presidential powers.
Arjomand's account cautions against reading the participatory provisions or branch structure apart from the Constitution's leadership framework.
This mixed allocation supports a qualified hybrid classification rather than a claim about Islam, Shi'a traditions, or Iranian society.

## vatican

The 2023 Fundamental Law vests ultimate legislative, executive, and judicial power in the Pope as sovereign while assigning ordinary functions to a legislative commission, the Governorate, and judicial organs.
As enacted, Article 8 limited the commission to a Cardinal President and other Cardinals; Leo XIV replaced that paragraph in November 2025 with a rule providing for the Cardinal and other members, including the President, all appointed by the Pope for five-year terms.
The Pope's Catholic office and state sovereignty are joined in one officeholder, but Vatican City remains institutionally distinct from the Holy See.
This supports a papal-theocracy classification of the state's formal design, not of Catholic people or Christianity, and it does not establish how every delegated power operates in practice.

## comparisons

Hierocracy asks whether priests rule; confessional-state analysis asks how a state privileges a religion; monarchy asks how the head of state is constituted; authoritarianism asks about pluralism and accountability; totalitarianism concerns a more encompassing project of domination.
Comparing specified powers avoids turning any of these labels into a judgment about a faith or its adherents.
