Imperialism can name a project, practice, relation, or explanatory theory for extending power beyond an existing political center.
It is not the same thing as a historically bounded empire, every unequal relationship, or an accusation detached from a demonstrated mechanism.

## meanings

Kohn and Reddy emphasize command through settlement, sovereignty, or indirect control.
Nkrumah instead argues that formally independent states can remain directed from outside through economic and policy mechanisms.
These definitions overlap but ask for different evidence and cannot be converted into a timeless country label.

## mechanisms

In Nkrumah's account, external control can survive independence through security footholds, privileges over territory, resources, and government functions, or the conditions attached to aid.
This is his diagnosis; applying it elsewhere requires evidence that a specified privilege operated in that relationship.

## theories

Capitalist theories connect imperial expansion to accumulation, while geopolitical, strategic, cultural, and state-centered explanations make rival predictions.
Civilizing language or cultural influence may belong to an imperial project, but rhetoric alone does not establish who could compel whom.

## bounded-practice

The Hawaiian annexation sequence shows direct territorial extension despite mass recorded opposition.
Bandung's communiqué opposed colonialism while defending sovereign equality, and Getachew reads related projects as attempts to remake international order rather than stop at isolated nation-states.
Nkrumah's later neo-colonial diagnosis makes Ghanaian legal independence and continuing economic autonomy separate research questions.

## comparisons

Ask whether the evidence concerns an empire's institutions, an actor's imperial program, a cross-border relation, or an attributed theory explaining it.
Cooper and Getachew both reject an inevitable passage from empire to isolated nation-states, while addressing different histories and political alternatives.
