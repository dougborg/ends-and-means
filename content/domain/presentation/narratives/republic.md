A republic is a constitutional or governmental-form claim, not proof that people participate equally or can hold rulers accountable.
Republicanism is a related family of political traditions, and neither term is interchangeable with democracy.

## meanings

Republic commonly distinguishes public government from hereditary monarchy.
Republican traditions ask additional questions about citizenship, public power, civic freedom, and popular rule.
A state can use the constitutional label while falling short of democratic inclusion or accountability.

## traditions

Contemporary republican theory often describes freedom as protection from arbitrary or uncontrolled power.
That attributed end is one interpretation within republican traditions, not a measured property inherited by every republic.

## founding-claims

Madison argued that republican authority should derive directly or indirectly from the people.
The 1787 Constitution nevertheless left House elector qualifications tied to state rules rather than establishing universal suffrage.
Popular derivation and inclusive political membership are therefore separate claims.

## bounded-comparisons

India's constitutional text calls the union a democratic republic and separately sets adult suffrage as the basis for elections.
Kahnawà:ke's participatory law-making shows the reverse boundary: democratic practices can be studied in community-specific terms without applying the republic label.

## disputes

The republic label leaves open who counts as the people, how officials are selected, which powers they hold, and how domination is prevented.
Those questions require institutional and historical evidence rather than inference from a name.

## comparisons

Compare republic with democracy by asking separately about constitutional form, political equality, selection procedures, accountability, and observed practice.
