# Project Cybersyn, 1971–1973 research memo

## Scope

This case is the incomplete Cybersyn project inside Chile’s state-controlled industrial sector, not Chile as a whole and not proof that computation resolves planning disputes.

## Findings

- `cybersyn-four-components`: the design joined telex communications, statistical software, simulation, and an operations room ([MIT Press Reader](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/project-cybersyn-chiles-radical-experiment-in-cybernetic-socialism/)).
- `exception-escalation-not-total-data-centralization`: the architecture intended to surface exceptions and direct limited central attention rather than duplicate all factory decisions centrally.
- `worker-participation-was-intended-not-completed`: Beer advocated worker modeling and access, while Medina emphasizes that the system and the political project were never fully realized ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525961/cybernetic-revolutionaries/)).
- `strike-use-was-bounded`: the telex network supported logistical reporting during strikes but could not overcome violence or the larger political crisis.

## Counterevidence and rival interpretations

The same design was presented as devolution by its advocates and feared as surveillance by opponents. Because intended worker participation remained incomplete, neither description should be promoted as the sole observed result. Emergency telex use also cannot validate the simulator, operations room, or an economy-wide planning mechanism.

## Limitations

- The project’s components had unequal maturity.
- The case ended through a military coup, not an internal technical evaluation.
- Crisis, foreign intervention, domestic opposition, and policy conflict confound performance judgments.
- Modern computational capacity changes feasibility but not automatically objectives, incentives, authority, or contestability.

## Duplicate search

The migrated framework mentions computational planning only as a broad variant and contains no bounded Cybersyn case. No staged proposal duplicates this entity.

## Recommended editorial decision

Accept as an intentionally difficult counterexample to a unitary command-planning category. Require any promotion to label intended participation separately from implemented authority and keep emergency use separate from long-run performance.
