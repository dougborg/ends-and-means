# Soviet industrial planning, 1928–1940 research memo

## Scope

This case covers the first three Five-Year Plans before wartime mobilization. It is not “the Soviet economy” as a timeless unit and does not stand in for all central-planning proposals.

## Findings

- `targeted-investment-and-industrialization`: Allen’s peer-reviewed simulation attributes rapid industrialization materially to heavy-industry investment and ambitious targets under soft budget constraints ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ereh/article-abstract/2/1/1/459122)).
- `planning-operated-through-bargaining-and-discretion`: Gregory’s archive-based study describes nested authority, planner-producer conflict, bargaining, hidden information, and substantial action outside formal plans ([Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-political-economy-of-stalinism/FAABB401D87807B17CFFE6FFD2FD83E6)).
- `collectivization-small-growth-contribution`: Allen estimates collectivization added little to growth relative to investment strategy and planning.
- `living-standard-estimates-disputed`: Davies’s expert review identifies materially different consumption estimates and measures, preventing a clean aggregate-welfare verdict ([Economic History Association](https://eh.net/book_reviews/farm-to-factory-a-reinterpretation-of-the-soviet-industrial-revolution/)).

## Counterevidence and rival interpretations

The packet retains both the industrialization result and its severe distributional and human-cost qualifications. Rapid catch-up may depend on initial underdevelopment and concentrated investment rather than the general superiority of administrative allocation. Conversely, evidence of bargaining and informal exchange shows how the system actually operated but does not prove that formal plans contributed nothing.

## Limitations

- Model-based counterfactuals are sensitive to assumptions.
- Aggregate output and consumption are not sufficient welfare measures.
- Planning and political coercion cannot be cleanly separated in this episode.
- The endpoint precedes the Second World War and later institutional changes.

## Duplicate search

The migrated framework contains a broad historical-evidence note for the USSR, not a bounded case record. No staged proposal duplicates this period-specific entity.

## Recommended editorial decision

Accept as a framework stress test after reviewers confirm the period boundary and wording of the welfare dispute. Do not convert it into a verdict on the central-planning tradition.
