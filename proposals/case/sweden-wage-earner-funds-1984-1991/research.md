# Research memo: enacted Swedish wage-earner funds

The 1983 government proposition is the authoritative source for formal design: five independent nine-member boards, government appointment, a worker-interest majority, regional grounding, payroll and profit-based finance, annual pension-system returns, an eight-percent company voting cap, and potential delegation of half the voting rights to local unions ([Sveriges riksdag](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/proposition/om-lontagarfonder_g70350/html/)).

Westerberg’s open-access peer-reviewed history supplies practice and outcome context. It reports that the funds held about 2.6 percent of exchange value by the end of 1991 and generally behaved more like conventional investment companies than controlling owners ([Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/enterprise-and-society/article/from-marxist-venture-to-venture-capitalists-the-swedish-wageearner-funds-and-the-market-turn-19831994/A3EA6060B9A66F27C32D1CEDC065CC04)). The 1991 proposition documents abolition at year-end and transfer to a liquidation board ([Sveriges riksdag](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/proposition/om-avveckling-av-lontagarfonderna_gf0336/html/)).

The central interpretive conflict should remain visible. Limited control may be read as dilution of an ownership-transfer project or as a safeguard against concentrated delegated power. The sources establish rules and behavior but cannot decide the weight readers give those Ends and Criteria.

The exact case is not canonical; it exists only in the non-canonical prototype. Recommended decision: approve the boundary and atomic empirical claims, retain both interpretations in any eventual trace, and verify locators during promotion.
