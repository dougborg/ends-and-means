# Research memo: Swedish Rehn–Meidner policy complex

The case is intentionally bounded from the 1951 LO program through the centralized-bargaining period ending in 1983. Erixon reconstructs the combined model and warns it was never consistently implemented ([author-access copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5097894_The_Rehn-Meidner_Model_in_Sweden_Its_Rise_Challenges_and_Survival)).

Molinder’s new wage series finds inequality stable until the late 1960s, rapid decline afterward, and evidence that deliberate union policy explains much of the 1970s compression; it also finds that compression coincided with declining rather than accelerating structural change ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ereh/article-abstract/23/1/97/4971549)). Hibbs and Locking distinguish a positive output/productivity association for reduced interindustry differentials from the lack of such evidence within industries ([University of Chicago Press](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/209976)).

These findings should be displayed together. They reject both a simple success story and a simple null result. The case cannot establish transferability, wealth redistribution, or a tradition-wide outcome. Duplicate search found no canonical case; the exact case remains present only in the non-canonical prototype and is therefore not silently merged.

Recommended decision: approve this boundary and its three atomic empirical claims for editorial reconciliation. Require locator checking against full text during promotion.
