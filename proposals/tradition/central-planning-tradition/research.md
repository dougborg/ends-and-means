# Central-planning tradition research memo

## Scope

This proposal tests whether the replacement framework can represent a tradition unlike social democracy without reverting to a country label or a single pro/con verdict. It treats planning as a coordination-and-authority family and asks which decisions are planned, by whom, using what information, with what revision and contest rules.

## Findings

- `planning-is-not-ownership` is an editorial classification: ownership and coordination are independent dimensions. This avoids treating every state-owned enterprise or public service as central planning.
- `historical-command-systems-had-informal-action` is supported by Paul Gregory’s archive-based account, which documents planner-producer conflicts and substantial action outside formal plans ([Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-political-economy-of-stalinism/FAABB401D87807B17CFFE6FFD2FD83E6)).
- `computational-planning-can-decentralize` is supported by Eden Medina’s history of Cybersyn, whose design combined national information flows with decentralized management and intended worker participation ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525961/cybernetic-revolutionaries/)).

## Counterevidence and rival interpretations

Kornai’s institutional account connects classical socialist bureaucratic coordination to persistent shortage and soft budget constraints ([Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/4729)). Gregory emphasizes principal-agent conflict, unreliable supplies, and planners’ limited knowledge. Medina’s case, however, shows that not every planning proposal sought the same concentration of operational authority. The contrast does not refute either account; it limits the domain to which each can be applied.

## Limitations

- Two cases cannot establish a comprehensive typology.
- Cybersyn was incomplete and operated inside a turbulent mixed economy.
- The Soviet case proposed alongside this dossier is limited to early industrialization; it cannot stand for the entire USSR.
- “Dominant” planning remains a classification threshold requiring explicit editorial judgment.

## Duplicate search

The existing migrated `centrally-planned-economy` is the intended predecessor. The proposed semantic ID changes the noun from an economy treated as an object to a tradition/design family. No other staged proposal represents this entity.

## Recommended editorial decision

Accept the model direction, subject to review of the renamed ID and scope boundary. Preserve hierarchical command planning, indicative planning, and participatory/computational planning as variants whose relationships and evidence must be established separately.
