# Research memo: social-democratic tradition

## Scope and findings

This is a revision proposal for the existing working dossier, not a new tradition. The Cambridge intellectual history supports treating social democracy as a historically important but internally changing attempt to place economic forces under democratic direction, rather than equating it with a single welfare program ([Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/17998/excerpt/9780521817998_excerpt.pdf)). Claim `social-democracy-historically-variable` therefore supports a family-level description while retaining contested boundaries.

Erixon’s peer-reviewed reconstruction identifies the Rehn–Meidner model’s combination of full employment, equity, price stability, and growth, but also stresses that it was never consistently implemented ([Journal of Economic Issues author-access copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5097894_The_Rehn-Meidner_Model_in_Sweden_Its_Rise_Challenges_and_Survival)). This supports the narrower editorial conclusion in `rehn-meidner-not-whole-tradition`: the Swedish episode can test institutional claims without standing in for social democracy everywhere.

## Counterevidence and rival interpretations

Definitions of social democracy differ across eras. Some emphasize gradual democratic transition toward socialism; others emphasize regulation and redistribution within a mixed capitalist economy. The proposed scope preserves this dispute through variants rather than selecting one era as the only authentic definition.

## Limitations

- This research packet is deliberately Sweden-heavy because it supports the first vertical slice.
- It does not establish claims about the performance of social democracy as a whole.
- The proposed Wikipedia URL is an orientation link, not evidence.
- Full dossier publication needs broader sources on non-Swedish and contemporary variants.
