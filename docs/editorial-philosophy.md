# Editorial Philosophy

**Status:** working principles; substantive value commitments still require
editorial discussion

Ends and Means is not written from nowhere. Choosing which systems to compare,
which problems count as cruxes, what evidence deserves weight, and what belongs
in a verdict all involve judgment. We will not describe those choices as neutral
when they are not.

Our standard is fairness, transparency, and corrigibility.

## Fairness, not viewlessness

Fairness means representing a system's aims and strongest serious arguments
before evaluating its mechanisms and record. It does not require giving every
claim equal weight, splitting every conclusion down the middle, or withholding
a judgment that the evidence supports.

We will distinguish:

- **Fact:** a claim that can be checked against cited evidence.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from facts, with its reasoning and
  uncertainty exposed.
- **Value judgment:** an assessment that depends partly on priorities such as
  liberty, equality, security, participation, prosperity, or human dignity.

A source can support a fact or document a viewpoint; it cannot make a value
judgment value-neutral.

## Evidence and disagreement

We aim to:

- follow evidence even when it cuts against our expectations;
- weigh sources by relevance and quality, not by whether they support a verdict;
- seek consequential counterevidence and credible competing interpretations;
- avoid false balance when evidence is materially asymmetric;
- distinguish an ideal model from institutions operating in a particular place
  and period;
- state when evidence is missing, contested, indirect, or unable to resolve a
  value disagreement;
- avoid generalizing from one historical case beyond what it can establish.

## Visible judgment

Verdicts are allowed, but they must be labeled, sourced, and explain their
criteria. Where reasonable people could reach different conclusions because
they prioritize different ends, the comparison should expose that dependency
rather than present one ranking as objective.

The project may eventually state substantive editorial commitments. Those
commitments should be adopted deliberately and published plainly, not inferred
after the fact from individual verdicts.

## Correction and accountability

Every important claim should become addressable. Readers should be able to see
its sources, challenge its interpretation, propose better evidence, and inspect
material revisions. Commercial relationships, including affiliate links, must
never determine source selection or evaluation.

We expect the work to change. A correction is evidence that the process can
learn, not an embarrassment to hide.

## Analysis and advocacy

The project can reach conclusions without becoming a campaign. Its primary job
is to make mechanisms, trade-offs, evidence, uncertainty, and value choices
legible. If a page advocates a position rather than comparing or analyzing it,
that change in purpose must be explicit.
