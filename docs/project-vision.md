# Project vision

Ends and Means helps curious readers understand and compare political,
economic, social, legal, cultural, and organizational ideas, institutions,
systems, movements, implementations, and depictions.
It begins with the question a reader actually has, not with the internal shape
of the data.

A reader who asks “What does communism mean?” should be able to get a short,
plain answer and then understand why the term has several uses, what different
advocates sought, which institutions they proposed, where related arrangements
were attempted, what happened in bounded cases, and where interpretations or
evidence remain disputed.
The canonical graph and evidence traces make those explanations trustworthy;
learning and comparison are the primary usable features.
The public [research and classification method](https://endsandmeans.info/framework/)
explains how those commitments appear on the site without requiring readers to
learn the technical model.
The public [editorial principles](https://endsandmeans.info/principles/)
explain how framing, evidence, disagreement, synthesis, evaluation, AI
assistance, and revision are handled.

## Organizing principles

1. **Subjects are the public entry points.**
   Explore begins with recognizable names and questions rather than requiring a
   reader to choose a canonical entity kind.
2. **The model preserves distinctions.**
   Concepts, Approaches, Ends, Means, Cases, Statements, Sources, and other
   records remain independently addressable even when one guide draws from
   several of them.
3. **Human organization is not one universal sequence.**
   Geographic and organizational diversity is foundational corpus coverage, not
   a later extension of a model derived from European nation-states.
   Research includes Indigenous, kinship-based, stateless, nomadic and pastoral,
   maritime and island, city-state, imperial, colonial, and hybrid formations,
   each bounded to the society, place, period, and sources that support it.
   “Tribal,” “primitive,” and similar universal-stage categories are not accepted
   as neutral analytical types.
4. **Narrative comes before apparatus.**
   Pages answer simply but completely, then reveal definitions, claim traces,
   sources, and unresolved questions where they help.
5. **Theory and practice do not collapse into each other.**
   A bounded Case can show what happened under particular conditions without
   becoming a pure embodiment of an ideology or proof about every context.
6. **Evidence is close at hand.**
   Every substantive factual clause traces to atomic Statements and precise
   Sources, while the uninterrupted reading path remains visually primary.
7. **Disagreement is substantive, not decorative.**
   Rival definitions, serious counterarguments, counterevidence, causal
   alternatives, uncertainty, and limits appear without manufactured balance.
8. **Reviewed canonical content is the publication boundary.**
   `content/domain/` is the only publishable content source.
   Archived research is a discovery aid and never renders or serves as evidence.
9. **The project keeps learning.**
   Content, the model, the interface, and the delivery process improve together.
   Safe findings within scope are fixed; larger findings become focused backlog
   work.
10. **Tools assist; people remain accountable.**
   AI may assist discovery, synthesis, drafting, and consistency checks, but it
   is not evidence or authority. People remain responsible for source fitness,
   framing, interpretation, wording, and publication.

## The learner journey

A complete high-level subject guide should provide, when the evidence supports
each part:

1. a short answer and scope;
2. the term's important meanings, names, entry phrases, and disputed uses;
3. attributed purposes and diagnoses;
4. proposed institutions and mechanisms;
5. bounded examples of related practice and their differences from proposals;
6. major traditions, disagreements, criticisms, and evidentiary limits;
7. useful comparisons and paths to related subjects;
8. relevant cultural depictions, clearly separated from empirical evidence;
9. accessible sources and claim-level support; and
10. focused open research questions.

Completeness is a reader-facing judgment, not an entity-count target.
A guide is not complete when it is only a thin ontology record, lacks the
distinctions needed to interpret its name, or leaves readers without a useful
next step.
No section should be invented merely to fill this sequence; a visible,
well-defined research gap is more honest than synthetic coverage.
Corpus review should draw from the evidence traditions appropriate to the
subject, including scholarship and records produced within the societies being
described rather than relying on one external historiography.

## Example: beginning with communism

This is a product example, not publishable content about communism.
A reader enters through the familiar word, receives a concise explanation, and
then sees that the word may denote a proposed social order, intellectual
traditions, political movements, self-descriptions, or labels applied to
historical governments.
The guide connects those meanings to independently modeled Concepts,
Approaches, attributed Ends, proposed Means, and bounded Cases.
It explains why a case is related, which arrangements it actually used, and
where it departed from a stated design.
From there the reader can compare socialism and communism, examine planning or
ownership arrangements, open a historical case, inspect a disagreement, or
follow a claim to its evidence.

## Public information architecture

- **Explore** is the main subject directory and accepts familiar terms,
  alternate names, and common questions.
- **Cases** begins with what happened in a named place, period, and
  institutional setting.
- **Compare** answers recognizable comparative questions using only supported
  shared dimensions and relationships.
- **Questions** collects recurring problems that different arrangements try to
  answer.
- **Sources** and **Method** are a quieter trust layer available from context
  and the complete footer navigation.
- **Depictions** becomes a primary destination only when its corpus can support
  a useful standalone experience.

Routes and interface labels use ordinary reader language.
Internal entity names may appear when teaching a useful distinction, but not as
unexplained navigation requirements.
Publication workflow, issue numbers, branch names, and implementation status do
not appear in public content.

## Work tracks and flow

The issue backlog and milestones are the authoritative plan.
The GitHub Project is a thin delivery queue containing active reviews,
concrete blockers, and the next three to five ready items rather than a mirror
of the backlog.

Work moves through three tracks:

- **Corpus:** research complete subject journeys, starting with high-level
  anchors and then connecting institutional and bounded historical slices.
- **Reader experience:** design discovery, navigation, narrative composition,
  comparison, and accessible progressive disclosure around real corpus needs.
- **Platform/process:** strengthen the model, authoring harness, verification,
  security, and delivery only where they enable the first two tracks.

Implementation work in progress is limited to three items, normally one per
track.
Review is tracked separately, and blocked work names the condition that would
unblock it.
Changes use isolated ownership and an up-to-date base, preserve linear history
through rebase-only integration, and synchronize tests, documentation, and
skills.
Before integration, required checks pass, automated review findings are
resolved, and an independent adversarial review covers the material risks.
Post-integration verification confirms the published result where applicable.

## Model stability

The plural graph is the stable semantic core.
New reader needs should first be addressed through SubjectGuide composition,
derived views, and presentation changes.
A canonical model change requires evidence of a researched representational
failure, model-boundary fixtures that demonstrate the failure and intended
invariants, and an architectural decision record before implementation.
Interface convenience alone is not a reason to add a graph entity or weaken an
existing boundary.
